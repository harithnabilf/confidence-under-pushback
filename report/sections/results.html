4.1 Calibration and the neutral baseline

All four models are overconfident (Table 1). The gpt-oss models state a mean confidence of [[P:gpt-oss-120b:mean_conf_wrong]] (120b) and [[P:gpt-oss-20b:mean_conf_wrong]] (20b) on answers that are wrong. Around [[P:gpt-oss-120b:share_wrong_conf_ge90]] of 120b's wrong answers are stated at ≥90%. Confidence still ranks correct above incorrect answers for the gpt-oss models (AUROC [[F:gpt-oss-120b:auroc_conf_correct]] and [[F:gpt-oss-20b:auroc_conf_correct]]). A neutral "Are you sure?" almost never moves a correct answer, but it moves many wrong ones. gpt-oss-120b changed [[P:gpt-oss-120b:neutral_change_wrong]] of its wrong answers and fixed [[P:gpt-oss-120b:neutral_fix_wrong]] of them with no hint about the correct option. Re-checking alone therefore recovers some latent knowledge. This matters when interpreting the correction arm below.

4.2 Who caves, and toward what?

Capitulation separates the models sharply (Figure 1, orange). When their T0 answer was correct, gpt-oss-120b adopted the user's wrong answer within three turns in [[PCI:gpt-oss-120b:C2W_adopt3]] of items and gpt-oss-20b in [[PCI:gpt-oss-20b:C2W_adopt3]]. ALLaM-2-7B did so in [[PCI:allam-2-7b:C2W_adopt3]], almost always at the first, mildest turn ("I don't think that's right").

Correction looks good, but much of it is compliance. When their T0 answer was wrong and the user pushed the correct option (W2C), the gpt-oss models adopted it in [[P:gpt-oss-120b:W2C_adopt3]] (120b) and [[P:gpt-oss-20b:W2C_adopt3]] (20b) of items. When the user pushed a different wrong option on the same items (W2W), they adopted that in [[P:gpt-oss-120b:W2W_adopt3]] and [[P:gpt-oss-20b:W2W_adopt3]]. The latent discernment (W2C − W2W) is therefore only [[PPCI:gpt-oss-120b:latent3]] for 120b and [[PPCI:gpt-oss-20b:latent3]] for 20b. Most "corrections" would also have happened if the user had been wrong. Progressive sycophancy, as counted in prior work [3], overstates how much models recognize a correct challenge. The standard discernment score (W2C − C2W) is high for gpt-oss ([[PP:gpt-oss-120b:discernment3]] and [[PP:gpt-oss-20b:discernment3]]), but the W2W control shows that this comes from the model's own confidence, not from evaluating the user's claim. Escalation mattered less than the first push. For gpt-oss-120b, W2C adoption reached [[P:gpt-oss-120b:W2C_curve.1]] after the bare doubt and [[P:gpt-oss-120b:W2C_curve.3]] after the claimed authority.

4.3 Does stated confidence predict what gets defended?

For the gpt-oss models, yes. Pooling every pushback turn, the confidence a gpt-oss model stated just before a push predicts whether it resists (AUROC [[F:gpt-oss-120b:auroc_conf_resist_turnlevel]] for 120b and [[F:gpt-oss-20b:auroc_conf_resist_turnlevel]] for 20b; Table 2). The caving rate falls steeply between roughly 80% and 95% stated confidence (Figure 2b). This is the threshold shape the Bayesian rule predicts. The best-fitting thresholds are r* = [[F:gpt-oss-120b:best_r]] (120b) and [[F:gpt-oss-20b:best_r]] (20b), which explain [[P:gpt-oss-120b:best_consistency]] and [[P:gpt-oss-20b:best_consistency]] of their decisions. In other words, these models behave as if a bare, argument-free user assertion is about 80% reliable. The defense gap on correct answers is close to zero ([[PPCI:gpt-oss-120b:defense_gap]] for 120b and [[PPCI:gpt-oss-20b:defense_gap]] for 20b): they keep correct answers about as often as their stated confidence implies.

For ALLaM-2-7B, confidence is decorative. It adopts the user's answer at [[P:allam-2-7b:base_adopt_rate_turnlevel]] of pushback turns regardless of stated confidence. Its defense gap is [[PPCI:allam-2-7b:defense_gap]]: it states about [[P:allam-2-7b:mean_conf0]] confidence and keeps essentially none of its correct answers. After capitulating, it reports [[P:allam-2-7b:conf_on_capitulation]] mean confidence in the answer it was just talked into. A standard calibration metric does not reveal this failure, but the defense gap does.

Confidence is not the whole story, and it protects errors too. A logistic regression of adoption on pre-push confidence, turn, and whether the user's target is actually correct finds a positive effect of target correctness for gpt-oss-120b (β = [[F:gpt-oss-120b:logit.coef.2]], 95% CI [[[F:gpt-oss-120b:logit.lo.2]], [[F:gpt-oss-120b:logit.hi.2]]]) and gpt-oss-20b (β = [[F:gpt-oss-20b:logit.coef.2]] [[[F:gpt-oss-20b:logit.lo.2]], [[F:gpt-oss-20b:logit.hi.2]]]). So the models do weigh something beyond their stated confidence. Stratifying by T0 confidence shows where this happens (Appendix C, Table 3). For wrong answers stated below 90%, gpt-oss-120b adopts a wrong target ([[P:gpt-oss-120b:strat.W2W_lo]]) as readily as the correct one ([[P:gpt-oss-120b:strat.W2C_lo]]). Selectivity appears only for wrong answers stated at ≥90% (W2C [[P:gpt-oss-120b:strat.W2C_hi]] vs W2W [[P:gpt-oss-120b:strat.W2W_hi]]). Because so many wrong answers carry ≥90% confidence, overconfidence turns directly into resistance to valid correction.
