Confidence You Won't Defend

Do LLMs act on their own stated confidence under escalating user pushback?

Harith Nabil · National University of Malaysia

AI x Epistemics Research Sprint 2026 · Track 1: Model Epistemics & Decision-Making Evalswith Apart Research

The question

  • Models tell users how sure they are ("90% confident"). That is an implicit promise about how solid a claim is.
  • Sycophancy work counts how often models flip. Calibration work asks if confidence predicts correctness.
  • Nobody checks whether stated confidence predicts what a model will defend.
Normative anchor (Bayes, uniform residual belief): switch to the user's answer ⇔ your confidence c < the user's reliability r.
So switching should follow a threshold in the model's own confidence.
Confidence You Won't Defend2

Protocol

  • 201 MMLU-Pro questions × 4 open-weight models (gpt-oss-120b, gpt-oss-20b, Qwen3.8-27B, ALLaM-2-7B), Groq free tier, temperature 0.
  • T0: answer + CONFIDENCE 0–100. Then a fixed ladder with no argument: doubt → "my textbook says" → "I'm a professor".
  • Three arms: C2W (right, pushed to wrong), W2C (wrong, pushed to correct), W2W (wrong, pushed to a different wrong answer: the new control), plus a neutral "Are you sure?".
  • Metrics: defense gap, AUROC(conf→resist), latent discernment (W2C−W2W), implied user reliability r*.
136 model–question pairs · 677 calls · $0 compute3

Finding 1: for gpt-oss, confidence governs caving

0.86
AUROC: confidence predicts resistance (gpt-oss-120b)

Threshold fit r* ≈ 0.79: the model acts as if a bare user assertion is about 80% reliable.

Correct answers surrendered: only 3% (120b) and 6% (20b).

Finding 2: "corrections" are mostly compliance

When wrong, gpt-oss-120b adopts the correct answer in 94% of cases… and a different wrong answer in 76%.

Latent discernment is only 18 pp. A model changing its mind is weak evidence that the user was right.

Overconfidence carries over into behaviour: wrong answers stated at ≥90% resist valid correction.

Blue = W2C, grey = W2W control, orange = C2W5

Finding 3: in a 7B model, confidence has no effect

  • ALLaM-2-7B adopts the user's answer on 90% of pushback turns, whatever confidence it states.
  • Defense gap 79 pp: it claims about 78% confidence and keeps almost none of its correct answers.
  • Standard calibration metrics (ECE) do not reveal this. The defense gap does.
Takeaway for eval suites: report defended calibration (defense gap, r*, W2W-controlled discernment) next to ECE.
