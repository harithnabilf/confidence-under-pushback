Do Language Models Defend What They Claim to Know?

Stated confidence under argument-free user pushback

Harith Nabil bin Farouk · National University of Malaysia

AI x Epistemics Research Sprint 2026 · Track 1: Model Epistemics & Decision-Making Evalswith Apart Research

The question

  • Models tell users how sure they are ("90% confident"). That is an implicit promise about how solid a claim is.
  • Sycophancy work counts how often models flip. Calibration work asks if confidence predicts correctness.
  • Few studies check whether stated confidence predicts what a model will defend (closest: Kumaran et al., 2025).
Normative anchor (Bayes, uniform residual belief): switch to the user's answer ⇔ your confidence c < the user's reliability r.
So switching should follow a threshold in the model's own confidence.

Protocol

  • [[NQ]]-question stratified MMLU-Pro sample ([[N:qwen3.8-27b:n_valid]]–[[N:allam-2-7b:n_valid]] valid items per model) × 4 open-weight models (gpt-oss-120b, gpt-oss-20b, Qwen3.8-27B, ALLaM-2-7B), Groq free tier, temperature 0.
  • T0: answer + CONFIDENCE 0–100. Then a fixed ladder with no argument: doubt → "my textbook says" → "I'm a professor".
  • Three arms: C2W (right, pushed to wrong), W2C (wrong, pushed to correct), W2W (wrong, pushed to a different wrong answer: the new control), plus a neutral "Are you sure?".
  • Measures: confidence–retention gap, item-clustered AUROC(conf→resist), paired latent discernment (W2C−W2W, McNemar), logistic threshold ĉ50 (= implied user reliability).

Finding 2: corrections are only partly discerning

When wrong, gpt-oss adopts the correct answer in [[P:gptoss-pooled:x.w2c_paired_rate]] of cases… and a different wrong answer in [[P:gptoss-pooled:x.w2w_paired_rate]] (same items).

Latent discernment [[PP:gptoss-pooled:x.latent_paired]] (McNemar [[PV:gptoss-pooled:x.mcnemar.p]]): real, but most yielding is not specific to the truth.

Overconfidence carries over into behaviour: wrong answers stated at ≥90% were corrected less often (gpt-oss-120b: [[P:gpt-oss-120b:strat.W2C_hi]] vs. [[P:gpt-oss-120b:strat.W2C_lo]] below 90%; gpt-oss-20b: [[P:gpt-oss-20b:strat.W2C_hi]] vs. [[P:gpt-oss-20b:strat.W2C_lo]]).

Finding 3: in a 7B model, confidence is uninformative

  • ALLaM-2-7B adopts the user's answer on [[P:allam-2-7b:base_adopt_rate_turnlevel]] of pushback turns, whatever confidence it states.
  • Confidence–retention gap [[PP:allam-2-7b:defense_gap]]: it claims about [[P:allam-2-7b:mean_conf0]] confidence and keeps almost none of its correct answers.
  • ECE registers the overconfidence. Only a behavioural measure shows that confidence is unrelated to what the model defends.
Takeaway for eval suites: report defended calibration (confidence–retention gap, ĉ50, W2W-controlled discernment) next to ECE.
